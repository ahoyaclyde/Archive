Privacy Policy

Latest update: March 15, 2025

1. Introduction: Our Commitment to Privacy and Justice

Welcome to Crime Bank, a community-powered evidence reporting platform that serves as a secure bridge between citizens and law enforcement. Working in tandem with FLUG (Forensic Learning and Augmentation Unit), our artificial intelligence system that enhances and analyzes forensic evidence, we have created a comprehensive ecosystem where ordinary citizens can contribute to community safety while maintaining complete control over their personal information.

This Privacy Policy is designed to be transparent, thorough, and accessible. It explains how Crime Bank operates as your trusted intermediary—collecting evidence submissions from the public, preserving them with blockchain-verified chain of custody, and when appropriate, routing them to law enforcement agencies or through FLUG for AI-powered forensic enhancement. We recognize that when you submit evidence to Crime Bank, you are placing tremendous trust in us. Whether you are a witness to a crime, a business owner with security footage, or a concerned citizen who captured something unusual on your dashcam, we take that trust seriously.

The relationship between Crime Bank and FLUG is fundamental to our service. Crime Bank acts as the secure vault and reporting interface where citizens submit evidence. FLUG, our Forensic Learning and Augmentation Unit, is the artificial intelligence engine that processes submitted evidence—enhancing blurry footage, identifying objects and individuals (with appropriate privacy safeguards), and finding connections across multiple submissions that human analysts might miss. Together, they form a complete forensic pipeline that has helped solve over 147 cases across 23 countries, all while maintaining the highest standards of data protection.

2. Understanding How Crime Bank and FLUG Work Together

Before diving into the details of data collection and processing, it helps to understand the basic flow of information through our system. When you submit evidence to Crime Bank—whether it's a video file, photograph, screenshot, or other digital evidence—that submission enters our secure intake system. At this point, you have choices about how your evidence is handled, who can access it, and whether you wish to remain completely anonymous or be available for follow-up contact.

Once submitted, evidence may be processed by FLUG, our forensic AI unit. FLUG performs several functions depending on the nature of the evidence and the permissions you've granted. It can enhance image and video quality, clarify blurry or pixelated footage, identify objects, vehicles, or individuals (with appropriate privacy filters applied), and compare submissions against patterns from known cases. Importantly, FLUG operates under strict privacy constraints—it can process evidence without ever revealing your identity, and all automated analysis is subject to human review before any action is taken.

Crime Bank maintains the secure evidence vault where all submissions are stored with cryptographic integrity verification. This vault is designed to meet court-admissible standards, meaning that evidence you submit today could be used in legal proceedings months or years from now, with complete chain of custody documentation. You retain control over your evidence throughout this process, with the ability to withdraw submissions (subject to legal holds if already matched to an active investigation) and manage access permissions at any time.

3. The Types of Information We Collect and Why

Understanding exactly what information Crime Bank collects is essential to making informed decisions about using our platform. We collect different categories of information for different purposes, and we are committed to transparency about each type.

3.1 Evidence Submissions: The Core of Our Service

When you submit evidence to Crime Bank, you are providing us with digital materials that may contain a wide range of information. This could include video files from security cameras, dashcams, or smartphones; photographs taken at the scene of an incident; screenshots of social media posts or messages; audio recordings; or documents related to a case. These files may contain images of people, vehicles, license plates, locations, and other potentially identifying information. They may also include metadata such as timestamps, GPS coordinates (if your device records them), camera settings, and file creation dates.

We collect this evidence because it is the entire purpose of Crime Bank—to serve as a secure repository where community members can preserve potentially crucial evidence and, when appropriate, share it with law enforcement. The FLUG AI system analyzes this evidence to enhance its quality and extract investigative leads that might otherwise remain hidden. For example, FLUG might take a grainy surveillance video and enhance it sufficiently to read a license plate, or it might identify that the same vehicle appears in two seemingly unrelated submissions, potentially linking separate incidents. All of this processing happens with strict attention to privacy and with human oversight.

3.2 Account Information: What We Know About You

If you create an account with Crime Bank, we collect certain information to establish and maintain your account. This typically includes your name, email address, and a password (which we store only in securely hashed form, never as plain text). You may also choose to provide additional information such as your phone number, organization affiliation (if you're submitting on behalf of a business or agency), or preferred contact methods. This information helps us communicate with you about your submissions, notify you if your evidence contributes to a resolution, and manage your account preferences.

Importantly, you are not required to create an account to submit evidence. Crime Bank offers completely anonymous submission options where no account information is collected at all. If you choose the anonymous route, your evidence is assigned a randomly generated identifier, and even we cannot trace it back to you. This is a core feature of our commitment to protecting citizen reporters who may fear retaliation.

3.3 Technical and Operational Data

Like any digital platform, Crime Bank collects certain technical information automatically when you use our services. This includes your IP address (which we may anonymize after a short period), browser type and version, device identifiers, operating system information, and pages you visit on our site. We also collect information about how you interact with our platform—what features you use, how long you spend on different pages, and any errors you encounter.

This technical data serves important purposes. It helps us maintain and improve the security of our platform by detecting suspicious access patterns or potential attacks. It allows us to optimize performance and user experience, ensuring that our services load quickly and work reliably across different devices. And it helps us understand how people use Crime Bank so we can focus our development efforts on the features that matter most. Where possible, we aggregate and anonymize this data so it cannot be linked back to individual users.

3.4 Payment and Transaction Information

For users who participate in our reward programs or subscribe to premium services, we collect payment information necessary to process transactions. This is handled by secure third-party payment processors who specialize in financial data security—we do not store credit card numbers or banking details on our own systems. We may retain records of transactions, including amounts and dates, for accounting and tax compliance purposes.

4. The Legal Foundations for Our Processing

Under data protection laws around the world, we must have a valid legal basis for processing personal information. Depending on the specific context and the type of data involved, Crime Bank relies on several different legal foundations.

4.1 When Processing Is Necessary for Our Contract With You

If you have an account with Crime Bank or have agreed to our terms of service, much of our processing is necessary to fulfill that contract. This includes creating and maintaining your account, processing your evidence submissions, providing you with access to your submitted materials, and communicating with you about your account and submissions. Without this processing, we simply could not provide the services you've requested.

4.2 When Processing Is Required by Law

As a platform that handles evidence potentially relevant to criminal investigations, Crime Bank must comply with various legal obligations. We may be required by law to preserve certain records for specified periods, to respond to valid legal process such as warrants or court orders, or to report certain types of evidence to authorities (for example, evidence related to imminent threats or crimes involving children). When we process information to comply with these legal obligations, we do so strictly as required and with appropriate oversight.

4.3 When Processing Serves Our Legitimate Interests

Like any organization, Crime Bank has legitimate business interests that justify certain processing activities, provided those interests are not overridden by your fundamental rights and freedoms. These legitimate interests include maintaining the security of our platform, improving our services, preventing fraud and abuse, conducting internal analytics and research, and establishing or defending legal claims. When we rely on legitimate interests, we carefully balance them against your privacy rights and provide you with the ability to object to such processing where appropriate.

4.4 When Processing Is Based on Your Consent

For certain processing activities, particularly those involving sensitive data or optional features, we ask for your explicit consent. For example, if you choose to participate in our reward program and provide identifying information to receive payment, we process that information based on your consent. If you opt in to receive marketing communications, we process your contact information for that purpose based on your consent. You have the right to withdraw consent at any time, and we make it easy to do so through your account settings or by contacting us.

4.5 When Processing Is in the Public Interest

Crime Bank's mission of supporting community safety and assisting in criminal investigations means that some of our processing serves the public interest. When we provide evidence to law enforcement agencies for authorized investigations, or when we use aggregated data to identify crime patterns that can inform public safety strategies, we are acting in the public interest. This legal basis is particularly relevant when we work with government agencies and law enforcement partners.

5. The FLUG Forensic AI System: How Artificial Intelligence Processes Evidence

FLUG (Forensic Learning and Augmentation Unit) represents the cutting edge of AI-powered forensic analysis, and we want you to understand exactly how it works with your evidence. FLUG is not a black box—it is a carefully designed system with extensive human oversight, privacy safeguards, and transparency features.

5.1 What FLUG Does With Your Evidence

When you submit evidence to Crime Bank, FLUG may process that evidence to enhance its forensic value. This processing can include several different types of analysis depending on the nature of the evidence and the permissions you've granted. FLUG can enhance image and video quality, reducing noise and clarifying blurry details to make evidence more usable for investigators. It can detect and classify objects—vehicles, weapons, specific clothing items, and other relevant features—helping to quickly identify what's present in footage. It can perform facial recognition (only when explicitly authorized and with strict privacy safeguards) to help identify suspects or victims. It can extract and read license plates from vehicles captured on camera. It can analyze audio for gunshot detection, voice identification, or other relevant sounds. And it can compare evidence across multiple submissions to identify patterns and connections that might link seemingly unrelated incidents.

All of this processing happens with the goal of helping investigators solve cases faster and more effectively. FLUG can process in minutes what would take human analysts days or weeks, and it can identify connections across thousands of submissions that no human could possibly keep track of. But importantly, FLUG never makes final decisions—its outputs are always reviewed by qualified forensic analysts before any action is taken.

5.2 How FLUG Protects Privacy During Processing

Privacy protection is built into FLUG's design from the ground up. When processing evidence, FLUG automatically detects and can redact personally identifiable information such as faces, license plates, and other identifying features unless explicit permission has been granted to process them. It operates with differential privacy techniques that add calibrated noise to prevent re-identification when data is used for training and improvement. It maintains strict access controls so that only authorized personnel with specific case assignments can view unredacted evidence. And it creates complete audit trails of all processing activities, so we always know who accessed what evidence and what analyses were performed.

For users who choose complete anonymity, FLUG processes evidence without ever associating it with any identifying information. The evidence is analyzed, but even if it yields a match or lead, we cannot tell you who submitted it—unless you've chosen to provide contact information separately. This is a deliberate design choice to protect citizen reporters who may face risks if their identity were known.

5.3 Human Oversight of Automated Decisions

FLUG is a tool to assist human investigators, not replace them. All significant outputs from FLUG—matches to other cases, identification of suspects, enhancement of evidence that might affect legal proceedings—are reviewed by qualified forensic analysts before any action is taken. These analysts have extensive training in both forensic methodology and privacy protection, and they serve as a critical check on automated processing. If FLUG flags a potential match between your evidence and an active investigation, an analyst reviews both pieces of evidence to confirm the connection before any notification is sent or evidence is shared.

We also maintain an appeals process for anyone affected by FLUG's analysis. If you believe that FLUG has made an error or processed your information improperly, you can contact our Data Protection Officer to request review and, if appropriate, correction or deletion of the affected data.

6. How Crime Bank Stores and Protects Your Evidence

The security of your evidence is our highest priority. Crime Bank employs a comprehensive set of technical, organizational, and physical safeguards designed to protect against unauthorized access, alteration, disclosure, or destruction of the information we hold.

6.1 Technical Security Measures

All evidence submitted to Crime Bank is encrypted both while in transit to our servers (using TLS 1.3 with perfect forward secrecy) and while at rest in our storage systems (using AES-256 encryption). Encryption keys are managed separately from the data itself, using hardware security modules (HSMs) in geographically distributed secure facilities. This means that even in the unlikely event of a data breach, the encrypted evidence would be unreadable without the keys, which are protected by additional layers of security.

Access to evidence is strictly controlled through role-based permissions and multi-factor authentication. Every access to evidence—whether by a Crime Bank employee, a FLUG processing system, or an authorized law enforcement partner—is logged with timestamps, user identification, and the specific actions taken. These audit logs are immutable and regularly reviewed for suspicious activity. We employ intrusion detection systems, 24/7 security monitoring, and regular penetration testing by independent security firms to identify and address potential vulnerabilities.

For particularly sensitive investigations, we offer air-gapped processing options where evidence is analyzed on systems with no network connectivity, ensuring that it cannot be accessed remotely under any circumstances. This is typically used for cases involving national security, ongoing major investigations, or other exceptionally sensitive contexts.

6.2 Organizational and Personnel Security

The people who work with your evidence are subject to rigorous screening and ongoing oversight. All Crime Bank employees and contractors undergo enhanced background checks, including criminal record verification and employment history review. They receive comprehensive training on privacy, security, and forensic handling procedures before being granted access to any systems, with annual refresher training and updates whenever procedures change.

Access to evidence is granted only on a need-to-know basis—meaning that employees can only access the specific evidence required for their assigned tasks, and only for as long as necessary to complete those tasks. We enforce strict separation of duties so that no single individual has uncontrolled access to evidence. All access is logged and regularly audited, with automated alerts for unusual access patterns.

6.3 Physical Security

Our data centers employ multiple layers of physical security, including biometric access controls, 24/7 video surveillance, intrusion detection systems, and on-site security personnel. Environmental controls maintain appropriate temperature and humidity levels, with redundant power and network infrastructure to ensure continuous availability. Only authorized personnel with specific, documented need are permitted physical access to server facilities, and all such access is logged and reviewed.

7. How Long We Keep Your Evidence and Why

Different types of evidence and information are retained for different periods, based on legal requirements, operational needs, and your preferences. We believe in being transparent about these timeframes so you can make informed decisions about what to submit.

7.1 Evidence During Active Investigations

If you submit evidence that becomes part of an active investigation, that evidence is retained for the duration of the investigation plus an additional period to allow for case closure activities, potential appeals, and related legal proceedings. Typically, this means evidence remains accessible to investigators for at least six months after a case is formally closed, though this can vary based on the complexity of the case and applicable legal requirements. During this period, access is restricted to the investigative team and authorized Crime Bank personnel supporting the investigation.

7.2 Completed Cases and Archival Storage

For evidence that contributed to a resolved case, we maintain archival copies for extended periods to support potential appeals, cold case reviews, and historical analysis. The standard retention period is seven years from case closure, though this may be longer in certain jurisdictions or for particular types of cases (such as those involving minors or particularly serious offenses). Archived evidence is stored with the same security measures as active evidence but is moved to cold storage with even more restricted access. It remains encrypted and chain-of-custody documented throughout this period.

7.3 Evidence Not Matched to Investigations

If you submit evidence that is not immediately matched to any active investigation, you have choices about how long it is retained. By default, such evidence is kept for two years, during which FLUG continues to compare it against new submissions and emerging case patterns—it might match something later even if it doesn't match now. You can choose to have your evidence retained for shorter or longer periods, or you can delete it at any time before it is matched to a case (once matched, legal holds may apply). You can also choose to have your evidence retained indefinitely as part of our reference database, helping to build pattern recognition capabilities that benefit future investigations.

7.4 Account Information and System Logs

If you have a Crime Bank account, your account information is retained as long as your account remains active. You can close your account at any time, and we will delete your personal information within 30 days, subject to any legal holds or requirements to retain records related to evidence submissions. System logs, which contain technical data about platform usage, are retained for two years to support security monitoring and incident investigation, after which they are anonymized or deleted.

7.5 Secure Deletion

When evidence reaches the end of its retention period or when you request deletion of evidence not subject to legal holds, we employ secure deletion methods that ensure the data cannot be recovered. This includes cryptographic erasure (destroying encryption keys so data becomes unreadable) for cloud storage, and physical destruction or Department of Defense-standard wiping for physical media. We provide certificates of deletion upon request for enterprise clients or particularly sensitive submissions.

8. When and How Crime Bank Shares Information

Crime Bank is built on the principle that you control your evidence. We do not sell your information to anyone, ever. We do not use your evidence for advertising or marketing. We share information only in carefully controlled circumstances and always with attention to your privacy and legal requirements.

8.1 Sharing With Law Enforcement

The primary purpose of Crime Bank is to help connect community evidence with law enforcement investigations. When you submit evidence, you can choose whether to make it available to law enforcement immediately, only after you review matching requests, or not at all (if you are simply preserving evidence for your own purposes). If you choose to make evidence available, it may be shared with authorized law enforcement agencies that are investigating cases potentially related to your evidence. Such sharing is always documented, with complete records of what was shared, with whom, and on what authority.

Law enforcement access to evidence is governed by strict protocols. Agencies must authenticate their identity and jurisdiction, specify the investigation for which evidence is sought, and agree to our terms of use governing handling and further sharing of evidence. All access is logged and you are notified when your evidence is accessed by law enforcement (unless such notification would compromise an active investigation, in which case notification occurs as soon as it is safe to do so).

8.2 Sharing With FLUG for Processing

As described above, evidence submitted to Crime Bank may be processed by FLUG, our forensic AI system. This processing is an integral part of the service and is covered by the permissions you grant when submitting evidence. FLUG operates within Crime Bank's security infrastructure and does not share evidence with any external parties except as part of authorized law enforcement sharing (described above) or with your explicit consent.

8.3 Sharing With Service Providers

Like any modern digital platform, Crime Bank relies on specialized service providers for certain functions. These include cloud infrastructure providers who host our data, payment processors who handle transactions, email service providers who help us communicate with users, and security firms who conduct audits and penetration testing. All such providers are carefully vetted, bound by strict data processing agreements that prohibit any use of your information beyond the specific services they provide, and required to maintain security standards at least as protective as our own.

8.4 Sharing Required by Law

In some circumstances, we may be required by law to disclose information. This could include response to valid warrants, court orders, or subpoenas; compliance with regulatory requirements; or reporting obligations related to specific types of crimes (such as those involving children or imminent threats). When we receive such requests, we review them carefully to ensure they are valid and appropriately scoped. We notify affected users when permitted by law, and we challenge requests that appear overbroad or invalid.

9. International Data Transfers

Crime Bank operates globally, and your evidence may be processed in countries different from where you are located. We take care to ensure that all international transfers of personal data comply with applicable laws and provide adequate protection.

For transfers from the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, or Switzerland to countries that have not been deemed adequate by the European Commission, we rely on European Commission-approved Standard Contractual Clauses, supplemented with additional safeguards as necessary. These clauses create contractual obligations on all parties to protect personal data to EU standards, regardless of where it is processed.

For transfers from other jurisdictions, we implement appropriate transfer mechanisms as required by local law. Where no specific mechanism is mandated, we apply the same high standards of protection to all transfers, ensuring that your information is protected by encryption, access controls, and contractual commitments wherever it goes.

Our primary data centers are located in the United States, the European Union, and Asia, with the ability to restrict processing to specific regions based on client requirements or legal obligations. For particularly sensitive investigations, we offer region-locked processing where evidence never leaves a specified jurisdiction.

10. Your Rights Over Your Information

Depending on where you live and the nature of your relationship with Crime Bank, you may have certain rights regarding the personal information we hold about you. We are committed to honoring these rights and making it easy for you to exercise them.

10.1 Right to Access

You have the right to know what personal information Crime Bank holds about you and to request a copy of that information. This includes not only the evidence you've submitted but also any account information, communications records, and other data we maintain that is linked to you. We will provide this information in a structured, commonly used format that you can transfer to another system if desired. To protect your privacy and the privacy of others, we may need to verify your identity before fulfilling access requests, and we may redact information that relates to other individuals.

10.2 Right to Rectification

If you believe that any personal information we hold about you is inaccurate or incomplete, you have the right to request correction. This could include updating your contact information, correcting a misattributed evidence submission, or fixing other errors. We will respond to such requests promptly and, where appropriate, notify any recipients of the corrected information.

10.3 Right to Deletion (Right to be Forgotten)

You have the right to request deletion of personal information we hold about you in certain circumstances. These include situations where the information is no longer necessary for the purposes for which it was collected, where you withdraw consent and no other legal basis applies, where you object to processing and there are no overriding legitimate grounds, or where the information has been unlawfully processed. This right is subject to important limitations—for example, we may need to retain information to comply with legal obligations, to establish or defend legal claims, or where the information is part of an active investigation subject to legal hold.

10.4 Right to Restriction

You have the right to request that we restrict processing of your personal information in certain situations. This could include while we verify the accuracy of information you've contested, where processing is unlawful but you prefer restriction to deletion, where we no longer need the information but you need it for legal claims, or while we evaluate an objection you've raised. When processing is restricted, we may still store the information but will not further process it without your consent or for legal purposes.

10.5 Right to Object

You have the right to object to processing of your personal information based on our legitimate interests or for direct marketing purposes. If you object to processing based on legitimate interests, we will cease processing unless we have compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests or the processing is for legal claims. If you object to direct marketing, we will stop immediately.

10.6 Right to Data Portability

You have the right to receive your personal information in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format and to transmit that information to another controller. This right applies to information you've provided to us based on consent or contract, and where processing is automated. We will provide this information in a standard format such as JSON or XML.

10.7 Rights Related to Automated Decision-Making

You have the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing that produces legal effects or similarly significantly affects you. As described above, all significant FLUG outputs are reviewed by human analysts, so decisions are not solely automated. However, if you believe you have been affected by an automated decision, you may request human review and contest the decision.

10.8 How to Exercise Your Rights

To exercise any of these rights, please contact our Data Protection Officer using the information in Section 15. We will respond to all valid requests within the timeframes required by law (typically 30 days, extendable by up to two months for complex requests). There is no charge for exercising your rights unless requests are manifestly unfounded or excessive. We may need to verify your identity before processing requests, and we may request additional information where necessary to locate the specific information you seek.

11. Special Considerations for Anonymous Submissions

One of Crime Bank's most important features is the ability to submit evidence completely anonymously. We want you to understand exactly how this works and what it means for your privacy.

When you choose anonymous submission, Crime Bank does not collect any identifying information about you. Not your name, not your email address, not your IP address (we anonymize it immediately), not any device identifiers. Your evidence is assigned a randomly generated identifier that cannot be linked back to you, and even we cannot trace who submitted it. This means that if you lose your submission ID, we cannot help you recover it—we genuinely don't know which submission was yours.

Anonymous submissions are processed by FLUG exactly like any other evidence. They may be matched to investigations, enhanced, and analyzed. If they contribute to a case resolution and a reward is available, we have systems for anonymous reward delivery—typically through cryptocurrency, gift cards, or other methods that don't require revealing your identity. You receive a one-time claim code at submission time that you can use to check status and claim rewards without ever identifying yourself.

The trade-off with anonymous submission is that we cannot contact you about your evidence unless you proactively check in using your submission ID. If your evidence becomes crucial to an investigation, we cannot reach out to ask follow-up questions or request additional information. For this reason, some users choose to provide limited contact information (like an anonymous email address) while otherwise remaining anonymous. This is a personal choice, and Crime Bank supports whatever level of anonymity you're comfortable with.

12. Children's Privacy

Crime Bank is not directed at children under the age of 18, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you are under 18, please do not use Crime Bank or submit any evidence through our platform. If we become aware that we have inadvertently collected personal information from a child under 18, we will take steps to delete that information promptly.

In some jurisdictions, the age threshold may be different (for example, 16 in some European countries). We comply with applicable local age requirements and will delete information collected from users below the relevant age in those jurisdictions.

If you are a parent or guardian who believes your child has submitted information to Crime Bank, please contact our Data Protection Officer immediately so we can investigate and take appropriate action.

13. Cookies and Similar Technologies

Crime Bank uses cookies and similar technologies to provide and improve our services, enhance security, and understand how our platform is used. Cookies are small text files stored on your device that help us recognize you and remember your preferences.

13.1 Essential Cookies

Some cookies are essential for the basic operation of Crime Bank. These include cookies that authenticate you when you log in, maintain your session as you navigate the site, remember your privacy preferences, and help balance traffic across our servers to ensure reliable performance. These cookies are necessary for the platform to function, and you cannot opt out of them while using Crime Bank (though you can block them through browser settings, which will prevent the platform from working properly).

13.2 Functional Cookies

Functional cookies remember choices you make to enhance your experience. For example, they might remember your language preference or save your submission preferences so you don't have to set them each time. These cookies are not strictly essential but make the platform more convenient to use.

13.3 Analytics and Performance Cookies

We use analytics cookies to understand how people use Crime Bank—which features are most popular, where users encounter errors, how the platform performs across different devices and locations. This information helps us focus our improvement efforts and ensure the platform works well for everyone. Analytics data is aggregated and anonymized wherever possible, and we use tools that respect user privacy (such as anonymizing IP addresses).

13.4 Managing Cookies

Most web browsers allow you to control cookies through their settings. You can usually find these settings in the "Options" or "Preferences" menu of your browser. Please note that disabling certain cookies may affect the functionality of Crime Bank—particularly essential cookies required for authentication and session management. You can also use browser extensions or privacy tools that provide more granular control over cookies.
